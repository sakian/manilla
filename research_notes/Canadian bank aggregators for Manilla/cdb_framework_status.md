# Canada's Consumer-Driven Banking (CDB / open banking) framework: status as of 2026-10-04, for a small self-hosted read-only budgeting app

Research date: 2026-10-04. Sources were fetched on that date. Where a claim comes only from a search-engine summary and not a fetched page, it is marked "(search snippet only, unverified)".

## 1. Timeline: legislation, Budget 2025 changes, draft regulations, final regs, coming into force

### Takeaway
The legal framework is now complete, but read access is **not usable yet**. The original Act (June 2024) was rewritten by Bill C-15 (Royal Assent 26 March 2026), which moved oversight from FCAC to the Bank of Canada. Draft regulations appeared in Canada Gazette Part I on 27 June 2026, with comments closing 26 August 2026 (both dates confirmed). Final regulations (Gazette Part II) had **not** been published as of early October 2026. They are meant to be fully in force within one year of Part II publication, with accreditation first, then the common rules, then product-by-product data scope. Realistically, mandated-bank read access for deposit accounts is a **2027** event, and lending and investment accounts come later. Write access is a separate, later legislative step: the government's target is to *legislate* it by mid-2027.

### Cited Findings
- The Consumer-Driven Banking Act was first enacted as Part 1 of the 2024 budget implementation legislation in June 2024. Bill C-15, the Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1, then received Royal Assent on **26 March 2026** and replaced or completed it. — [DLA Piper, 22 Apr 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-pl/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained); [Blakes, 3 Jul 2026](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)

### Inferences
- Part II publication had not happened as of 2026-10-04: the Gazette Part II search found nothing, and law-firm and Bank of Canada pages still describe the rules as proposed. If Part II comes in roughly Q4 2026 to Q1 2027, and accreditation comes into force first, then the common rules and the deposit/payment data scope would land somewhere in 2027 at the earliest. Lending and investment data would follow later. **No official date exists.**
- Several aggregator and secondary sites still say "Phase 1 in early 2026", for example the [search summary citing openbankingtracker](https://www.openbankingtracker.com/country/canada). That is stale: it reflects pre-Budget-2025 expectations, and no government source supports it now.
- "Mid-2027" is a target for *legislating* write access, not for launching it.

### Gaps
- No official expected date for final regulations (Part II) or for any phase going live. I found no Finance or Bank of Canada statement giving one.
- The text of the Advisory Committee's 21 July 2026 summary could not be read: it is a PDF and no extractor was available.
- A search summary claimed that on **5 September 2026** the Bank of Canada "outlined registry and rollout architecture with new advisory notes clarifying product phasing". I could not find a primary source for this, and the page cited for it was dated May 2026. Treat it as **unverified**.

## 2. Which institutions must participate, and phasing by account type

### Takeaway
Participation is mandatory only for large banks above a "retail volume" threshold that the Minister designates. The threshold has **not been published**. Secondary sources assume the Big Six, but no primary source names them. Everyone else, including other federal institutions, provincial credit unions and Desjardins, Crown corporations, PSPs and fintechs, opts in through accreditation. Data scope phases in by product: deposit and payment accounts first, then lending, then registered and then non-registered investment accounts. Credit cards are not named explicitly; they most plausibly fall under "payment products" or unsecured "lending", and which one decides whether they are in the first wave.

### Cited Findings
- "Participation in the framework will initially be mandated for specified large banks based on a threshold for retail volume." The RIAS gives no figure and no list of banks. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- "Remaining federally regulated financial institutions, as well as credit unions, crown corporations ... would be able to opt in, provided they meet the requirements." — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- "Schedule I banks may be designated by the Minister of Finance for mandatory participation, while other entities may seek accreditation on a voluntary basis." — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-pl/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained)
- "Large banks above a retail volume threshold" must participate, but "the specific threshold hasn't been published". — [Flinks blog, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions)
- Mandated banks do not go through accreditation but are "fully subject to the so-called 'common rules'". — [McMillan via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/financial-services/1814068/consumer-driven-banking-new-details-set-out-in-draft-regulations); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- The Big Six (RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC, National Bank) are said to be mandatory in Phase 1, with Desjardins and EQ opting in. This is a secondary claim from a search summary; neither the Gazette nor any law firm I read confirms it.
- Data scope covers "deposit accounts (chequing and savings), payment products, investment accounts (registered and non-registered), and lending accounts (secure and unsecured)". — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); same list on [Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/open-banking-implementation/budget-2025-canadas-framework-for-consumer-driven-banking.html)
- Order of phasing: "deposit and payment accounts would come into scope first, followed by lending accounts, registered accounts and non-registered accounts." — [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canadas-consumer-driven-banking-framework-takes-shape-with-draft-regulations); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Data must include "at least 24 months of consumer data" on request. — [Bennett Jones, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations). Three categories: consumer profile, account and product data. "Derived data that has been significantly enhanced" is excluded. — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- API endpoints must be available "99.5% of the time each month". — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations). Rate limiting is allowed only for stability or security, and must be "proportionate and non-discriminatory". — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations)
- Credit cards: the Gazette RIAS, Blakes, Bennett Jones, McMillan and Norton Rose do not mention them explicitly. — (sources above)

### Inferences
- A credit card is commonly treated as a "payment product". If so, it would be in the first wave with deposit accounts. If it is classed as unsecured lending, it waits for the second wave. **This is unresolved**, and it matters for a budgeting app.
- In the RIAS sensitivity analysis, about 680 affected businesses corresponds to a 25% first-year opt-in rate ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)). That suggests credit unions and smaller banks may come slowly, so coverage beyond the mandated banks is likely to be patchy at launch.

### Gaps
- The retail-volume threshold and the list of mandated banks. Neither appears in the RIAS summaries I could access, and the operative regulatory text was not fully extracted.
- Durations for each product phase (for example "N months after"). No source gives them.
- How credit cards are classified.

## 3. Accreditation of third parties: requirements, tiers, and whether a small developer can realistically take part

### Takeaway
An app that receives data must be an accredited "participating entity". There are four pathways: (1) federal or provincial financial institutions; (2) a streamlined path for PSPs registered under the RPAA; (3) a non-streamlined path for fintechs and others; and (4) accredited third-party service providers (ATPSPs), which carry out consent, authentication or data-movement functions *for* participating entities. The non-streamlined path requires a place of business in Canada, insurance or equivalent, independent third-party confirmation of security safeguards, complaints procedures, consent management, good-character vetting of key people, and conformance to the technical standard. It costs a $2,500 application fee plus annual assessment fees from $10,000. I found **no personal-use, self-access or small-entity exemption**, and no explicit provision letting a non-accredited "fourth party" receive data through an accredited aggregator. For a self-hosted hobby app, direct accreditation is unrealistic. Whether the aggregator route works under CDB rules is an open legal question; in practice, today's route is a commercial aggregator operating under bilateral contracts outside the framework.

### Cited Findings
- **Four pathways:** federal/provincial FIs (a declaration of compliance and good standing with their regulator); RPAA-registered PSPs (streamlined); non-streamlined "other entities" (fintechs); and ATPSPs. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations)
- **Non-streamlined requirements:** "a place of business in Canada". Applicants must show that "insurance or comparable guarantees cover risks associated with the management of consumer-driven banking data". They must provide evidence of "technical standard adherence, complaints procedures and consent management processes". They need "a policy under which all individuals with significant responsibility ... are regularly assessed for integrity and good character". They must meet "baseline security requirements, including incident management ... system and application security ... data protection and encryption". — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- Applicants also need "independent third-party confirmation of their security safeguards". — [McMillan via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/financial-services/1814068/consumer-driven-banking-new-details-set-out-in-draft-regulations). Section 37 of the drafts lists controls including vulnerability remediation, multi-factor authentication and encryption. — [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canadas-consumer-driven-banking-framework-takes-shape-with-draft-regulations)
- The regulations "would not prescribe a specific" insurance amount. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- **Fees:** the accreditation application fee is "$2,500, to be adjusted for inflation and rounded to the nearest $100 on a yearly basis". Annual assessment fees for participating entities are tiered base fees "ranging from $10,000 up to $150,000", plus variable amounts. ATPSPs pay a flat "$10,000" a year, and the external complaints body pays "$50,000". — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations)
- **Compliance cost in the RIAS:** about 680 affected businesses, of which 86% (578) are small businesses. The "average annualized cost per small business" is **$89,133**. The total cost is $457.7M PV over 10 years, against $13.2B of benefits. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- **Outsourcing:** participating entities that "outsource consent management, authentication, or data-movement functions [must] use only accredited providers". — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-pl/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained). The RIAS describes ATPSPs as entities that "may assist participating entities by carrying out certain activities on their behalf". — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- **Fourth parties:** the RIAS "does not address onward disclosure to non-accredited third parties or 'fourth parties'". Norton Rose says the regulations "do not explicitly address fourth parties or agent disclosures". Bennett Jones found no provision letting a non-accredited entity receive data. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations); [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations)
- **No exemptions:** none of the documents reviewed mentions accreditation of natural persons, a personal-use exemption or a size-based exemption. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations) ("the regulations do not define size-based exemptions")
- Before sharing data, entities must verify the counterparty's identity and "confirm, with reference to the Bank's registry, that the other entity's accreditation has not been suspended or restricted". — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations)
- The Bank of Canada will "evaluate applications and publish the list of accredited participating entities in a central public registry". — [Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/open-banking-implementation/budget-2025-canadas-framework-for-consumer-driven-banking.html)
- **Consent:** express consent lasts at most 12 months and must then be renewed. — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/). Consent records are kept for 5 years. — [Flinks](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions)
- **Penalties:** up to C$1M for individuals and C$10M for participating entities or ATPSPs. — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations)
- National security review: the Minister of Finance may review accreditation applicants. — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- Flinks calls itself an "access and accreditation partner, vetting fintechs seeking API connections". — [Flinks](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions)
- The OPC's 26 August submission asked for tighter accreditation: the Bank should be satisfied that ATPSPs have adequate security, with independent verification, and should require complaint-procedure information and good-character assessments. — [OPC](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/submissions-to-consultations/sub_fc_20260826/)

### Inferences
- **Direct accreditation is out of reach for a one-person self-hosted app.** It needs a Canadian place of business, insurance, an independent security attestation, a complaints process and membership of the external complaints body, plus at least $2,500 up front and $10,000 or more a year. Ongoing compliance averages about $89K a year for a small business. An individual running software for their own household is not the kind of entity the framework contemplates.
- **The ATPSP category is built for the other direction:** it serves accredited entities and does not stand in front of unaccredited apps. Under the drafts as summarized, an aggregator such as Flinks or Plaid could be an ATPSP for an accredited fintech, or be accredited itself as a participating entity. Nothing found says an accredited aggregator may pass CDB data on to an unaccredited downstream app. **That is the key open question for Manilla.**
- In practice, through 2026 and probably most of 2027, a small app will get Canadian bank data only through commercial aggregators (Plaid, Flinks, MX and so on), using their existing bilateral API agreements or screen scraping, which both remain legal. Alternatively, it can import the bank's own export files, which needs no third party at all. That last point is my inference; it was not researched here.
- I found no CDB mechanism that lets a consumer pull their own data through the framework's APIs for personal use. The framework is designed purely as bank-to-accredited-entity sharing.

### Gaps
- Whether the final regulations will allow, or bar, onward disclosure from an accredited entity to non-accredited "fourth parties". Not addressed in any source found.
- The insurance amount, which the drafts deliberately leave unprescribed. The Bank of Canada guidelines that may set expectations have not been published.
- When the Bank of Canada will open accreditation applications. No date has been published.

## 4. Technical standard, standards body, and sandbox

### Takeaway
The Act requires a single technical standard, set by a technical standards body that the Minister of Finance designates. **As of October 2026 no body has been designated**, and the draft regulations do not name FDX. FDX is the de facto industry standard in Canada: FDX Canada has existed since 2020, and the big banks' existing aggregator APIs largely use it. It is widely expected to be chosen, but that remains a projection. I found no government sandbox or conformance environment.

### Cited Findings
- "The Act requires participating entities to implement a single technical standard set by a technical standards body." The designation criteria include being "meaningfully Canadian; having a governance structure that is fair, open, and accessible ... with independent decision making". — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- The Gazette RIAS has no mention of FDX, a sandbox or a testing environment. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- "The Minister of Finance is expected to designate: The technical standards body." This is still future tense as of July 2026. — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/). The regulations prescribe what the body's annual report to the Bank must contain. — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations)
- The standards body is "unnamed as of publication". — [Flinks, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions)
- FDX "has not yet achieved any formal recognition in Canada, in spite of strong industry adoption ... FDX is a likely choice." — [Ozone API open-finance tracker](https://ozoneapi.com/the-open-finance-tracker/library/consumer-driven-banking-open-banking-canada/) (secondary, undated)
- FDX Canada launched on **29 July 2020** with 31 initial members, including BMO, CIBC, Desjardins, RBC, TD, National Bank, Tangerine and Equitable Bank. Its working group was co-chaired by Interac and TD. — [Newswire / FDX press release](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/leading-canadian-financial-services-firms-moving-to-adopt-the-fdx-technical-standards-for-secure-financial-data-sharing-859396367.html)
- Advisory Committee topics on 21 July 2026 included "designated bodies" and the "trust framework". — [Bank of Canada Advisory Committee page](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/regulatory-oversight/consumer-driven-banking/consumer-driven-banking-advisory-committee/)

### Inferences
- Once a body is designated, accreditation will require conformance to its standard. That will probably include the security profile (FAPI/OAuth) and certification, which adds more cost for any non-institutional participant.
- FDX's API specification is open to read, but a hobby app cannot use it against Canadian banks: the bank endpoints are only open to contracted aggregators or partners.

### Gaps
- No designation of the technical standards body has been announced, and no date for one has been given.
- No sandbox or conformance environment, from the government or the Bank of Canada, was found. I did not find any FDX Canada-specific CDB conformance program announced in 2026.

## 5. Screen scraping and consumer liability

### Takeaway
The Act contains a ban on screen scraping, but it is **not in force**. Government and law firms all say it will be brought in only after further consultation, once the framework is fully operational. Screen scraping therefore stays lawful during the initial rollout. Consumers are generally not liable for losses from data sharing within the framework unless they were grossly negligent with their credentials. Liability "flows with the data" between participants.

### Cited Findings
- "The Act includes a prohibition on screen scraping that is not required to operationalize the broader framework and will not be brought into force until broader consultation and policy development have taken place." — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- "About nine million Canadians currently access financial data sharing services by providing their confidential banking credentials in a process known as screen scraping." — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- "The prohibition of screen scraping will come into force once the framework is fully operational." The Department will "determine an appropriate timeline". — [Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/open-banking-implementation/budget-2025-canadas-framework-for-consumer-driven-banking.html)
- "Screen scraping remains permitted during the initial rollout." — [Flinks](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions). "The prohibition on screen scraping will not come into force as part of the initial implementation." — [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)
- The Act "enacts a prohibition on screen scraping, which is now considered an offence". — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-pl/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained). This describes the enacted-but-not-commenced provision; other sources make clear it is not yet in force.
- **Liability allocation:** requesters are responsible for consent and for receiving data securely; providers are responsible for authenticating the consumer and providing data securely. "Liability flows with the data." — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)
- Consumers are not liable "as long as they have not been grossly negligent" in protecting authentication information. — [Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/open-banking-implementation/budget-2025-canadas-framework-for-consumer-driven-banking.html); "gross negligence (or gross fault in Quebec)" — [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canadas-consumer-driven-banking-framework-takes-shape-with-draft-regulations)
- "A participating entity is liable to the consumer for any financial loss that directly results from the loss, unauthorized access, or unauthorized use of the consumer's data." — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-pl/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained)
- An independent, not-for-profit external complaints body, designated by the Minister and overseen by the Bank, will resolve CDB complaints free of charge. — [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)

### Inferences
- Credential-sharing screen scraping, by an aggregator or by the user's own script, carries no CDB liability protection. A bank's own terms may still treat sharing credentials as a breach that shifts fraud liability onto the customer. That is outside this research and not verified here.
- Banning scraping is likely to come at least a year or two after launch. Until then, aggregators will keep scraping banks that have no API.

### Gaps
- No date, consultation timetable or draft for bringing the scraping ban into force.

## 6. Bilateral bank–aggregator agreements and live APIs today

### Takeaway
Before CDB there were private, bilateral "open banking" API agreements between the big banks and aggregators, most from 2020 to 2023. RBC has agreements with Plaid and Yodlee (2022), TD with Finicity (2020) and Plaid (Dec 2023), CIBC with MX (Aug 2022), and National Bank with Flinks (2021; National Bank is Flinks' majority owner). EQ Bank works with Flinks. I found no public agreement announcement for Scotiabank. For BMO, the record shows a history of cutting off aggregator access, and I found no clear public agreement. Plaid and Flinks list all the Big Six, Desjardins and others as covered, but coverage may mix API and scraping. These agreements are the only API route that works today, and only for an aggregator's customers.

### Inferences
- These agreements are aggregator-only. The banks give API access to the contracted aggregator, not to that aggregator's small customers directly. A small app gets the benefit only by becoming a paying aggregator customer, and has to pass that aggregator's own vetting (Flinks: "vetting fintechs").
- Desjardins is a major Quebec institution and is provincially regulated, so it is an opt-in participant under CDB. I found no public bilateral API agreement for it.
