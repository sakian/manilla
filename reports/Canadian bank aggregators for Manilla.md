# Build against Plaid's sandbox, connect nothing yet

> Written 4 October 2026, before the decision to accept a credential-based
> connection until open banking arrives (see docs/measurements.md and #10).
> Its advice to refuse linking until a provider shows a per-connection API signal
> is superseded by that decision; the rest is the research it was based on.

The decision in issue #10 still holds. As of October 2026, **no aggregator reaches Canadian personal accounts through anything but the customer's own credentials**. Plaid's documentation says OAuth "is not currently used by financial institutions in Canada", and every Canadian institution on Flinks' demo instance reports `IsOauth: false`, National Bank (Flinks' own majority owner) included. Any live connection made today would be the screen scraping Manilla chose to refuse. When the consumer-driven banking (CDB) framework opens, **Plaid is the aggregator to target first**. It is the only one that a single household can use in production with no company, no contract and no fee: its new free Trial plan gives 10 production Items, which is plenty for a household. It also has the data model that best fits FR-17 to FR-19: cursor-based deltas, `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, and a link from each posted transaction back to its pending one. Flinks is better placed politically, but costs at least C$500 a month. MX, through the US$15-a-year SimpleFIN Bridge, is the cheap fallback, but it has a weaker data model and dates sent as epoch timestamps. The useful work now is an aggregator-neutral sync core in `src/`, tested against Plaid's sandbox and Flinks' public sandbox, neither of which carries real data. Two things decide whether the plan works at all, and neither is settled yet. The first is whether the final regulations let an accredited aggregator pass CDB data on to an unaccredited app like Manilla. The second is whether credit cards count as "payment products" and so arrive in the first wave.

## Every aggregator still signs in as the customer

The private API agreements Canadian banks signed between 2020 and 2023 make coverage look better than it is. RBC signed with Plaid and with Yodlee in June 2022 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/rbc-and-plaid-announce-agreement-to-bolster-client-security-and-increase-connection-to-financial-services-apps-301567535.html); [Newswire](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/rbc-and-envestnet-data-and-analytics-announce-agreement-to-provide-clients-with-greater-control-over-their-financial-data-830484763.html)). TD signed with Finicity in 2020 and with Plaid in December 2023 ([TD Stories](https://stories.td.com/ca/en/news/2023-12-14-td-bank-group-and-plaid-enter-into-north-american-data-acces)). CIBC signed with MX in August 2022 ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/cibc-latest-big-six-bank-to-partner-with-a-fintech-on-private-open-banking-api/)), and National Bank built an "open banking environment" with Flinks in 2021 ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/tired-of-waiting-flinks-launches-its-own-open-banking-environment-with-national-bank/)). No public agreement was found for Scotiabank, BMO or Desjardins. None of these agreements shows up where a developer can see it. Plaid's own OAuth guide, fetched on 4 October 2026, says OAuth **"is not currently used by financial institutions in Canada"** ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/link/oauth/)). A live query of Flinks' institution search returned `IsOauth: false` and a username/password form for all of the Big Six, Desjardins, Tangerine, Simplii, EQ, Amex, Rogers, PC Financial and the major credit unions ([Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/guides/connect/supported-institutions)). That query hit Flinks' demo instance, and a production instance might route some banks differently. Even so, both vendors describe the same reality.

That reality is fragile. Flinks warns that "most Canadian institutions now apply MFA on every login, so a significant share of connections will not refresh on their own" ([Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/guides/connect/nightly-refresh)). Lunch Money's tracker of Plaid's known issues for July to September 2026 lists failures at **BMO, CIBC, RBC, Tangerine, PC Financial, Coast Capital, Canadian Tire Bank and Wealthsimple**. These include MFA being invalidated right after setup, which breaks background updates, and "frequent relinking" at RBC ([Lunch Money](https://support.lunchmoney.app/guides/automatic-imports/institution-specific-issues)). The breakage comes from the credential-based connections underneath, not from any one app. YNAB and Monarch, which can route each bank through a different provider, still flag Canadian banks as the weak spot ([Monarch](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048393352-Guide-to-Connecting-Your-Accounts)).

The best case against waiting is what Actual Budget users already do: pay SimpleFIN US$15 a year and sync through MX ([Actual Budget](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/simplefin/)). It is cheap, it works for many people, and SimpleFIN never sees the password, because MX holds it ([SimpleFIN](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/info/security)). But that is exactly the arrangement docs/measurements.md rejects: a third party holding the household's banking login. CDB does not make it safer either. The framework's consumer protection, under which a consumer is not liable unless grossly negligent, applies to data shared *within the framework* ([Finance Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/open-banking-implementation/budget-2025-canadas-framework-for-consumer-driven-banking.html)). Scraping falls outside it. The scraping ban is enacted but will not come into force until the framework is fully operational ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)), so until then the market will keep offering scraped connections that look like a feature.

## The framework opens in 2027 at the earliest, and "live" is not enough

Bill C-15 rewrote the 2024 Act. It received Royal Assent on **26 March 2026** and moved oversight to the Bank of Canada ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-pl/insights/publications/2026/04/the-new-consumer-driven-banking-act-explained)). Draft regulations appeared in Canada Gazette Part I on **27 June 2026**, and comments closed on 26 August ([McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canadas-consumer-driven-banking-framework-takes-shape-with-draft-regulations)). Final regulations (Part II) had not been published by early October 2026. They are to be fully in force within a year of publication, with accreditation first and the common rules after it ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)). The Gazette text gives **no coming-into-force dates at all**. Read access to deposit accounts at the mandated banks is therefore realistically a 2027 event. Flinks' blog still says "Phase 1 in 2026" ([Flinks](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions)), which predates the slip and should not be relied on.

Two details in the drafts matter more to Manilla than the launch date. The first is the order of data scope: **deposit and payment accounts first, then lending, then registered and non-registered investments** ([McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canadas-consumer-driven-banking-framework-takes-shape-with-draft-regulations)). None of the regulatory summaries says where credit cards fall. If a card counts as a payment product, it arrives with chequing and savings. If it counts as unsecured lending, it waits a phase. For an envelope budget, where most spending goes through a card, that decides whether the first wave is useful at all. The second is that mandatory participation applies to banks above a retail-volume threshold that has **not been published**, and other institutions, Desjardins and the credit unions included, opt in ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)). The regulatory impact analysis models a 25% first-year opt-in rate, so expect coverage beyond the large banks to be patchy at launch.

Manilla cannot be a participant itself. A non-institutional applicant needs a Canadian place of business, insurance, an independent security attestation, a complaints process and good-character vetting of its key people. It pays a **$2,500 application fee and at least $10,000 a year** in assessments, and the impact analysis puts average compliance cost for a small business at **$89,133 a year** ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)). There is no personal-use or size exemption ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/83263b6e/open-banking-in-canada-key-insights-into-the-proposed-regulations)). That leaves the route issue #10 assumed: an accredited aggregator, with Manilla as its customer. The drafts are silent on exactly that. The impact analysis "does not address onward disclosure to non-accredited third parties or 'fourth parties'" ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html)), and Bennett Jones found no provision letting a non-accredited entity receive data ([Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations)). The accredited-service-provider category runs the other way: those providers work *for* accredited entities. If the final text leaves this unaddressed or forbids it, an aggregator could carry CDB data for its accredited customers while still serving hobby apps through its older, credential-based pipes. "The framework is live" and "Manilla can receive framework data" are separate events, and the plan depends on the second.

Two small corrections to docs/measurements.md follow from this. The Act now in force is the March 2026 rewrite, not the June 2024 original. And which banks "must participate from the outset" depends on a threshold nobody has published yet.

## Plaid is the pick; Flinks is better placed but priced for businesses

| Aggregator | Canadian reach today | How one household gets production access | Fit with FR-17 to FR-19 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plaid** | All major institutions, credential-based. RBC and TD agreements | Free Trial plan, 10 lifetime Items, self-serve | Cursor deltas, pending linked to posted, `YYYY-MM-DD` dates | **Primary target** |
| **Flinks** | All major institutions, credential-based. National Bank owns it. Intends to seek accreditation as a service provider | C$500/month minimum, 1-year term | Posted only, 90 or 365-day snapshots, no deltas | Sandbox only, unless pricing changes |
| **MX** | CIBC agreement. Canadian deposit coverage claimed but unlisted | Free dev tier at "top institutions". Production through sales | `guid`, pending/posted status, `updated_at` filter, but pending usually re-minted on posting | Watch. Reachable via SimpleFIN |
| **SimpleFIN Bridge** | Whatever MX covers. No Canadian list published | US$15/year, setup token | Per-account `id`, pending flag, epoch dates, no deltas | Fallback adapter |
| **Lunch Flow** | Routes across MX, Finicity and others | About US$35/year for 2 connections (unverified) | Abstraction over several providers | Watch |
| **Finicity / Mastercard** | Published API is US-only | Sales | Date windows, no deltas | Rule out |
| **Yodlee** | RBC agreement. Docs list US, UK and Australia | Sales. Sandbox no longer links live sites | New ID on posting by default | Rule out |
| **Salt Edge, Akoya, Teller** | Negligible or US-only | — | — | Rule out |
| **Inverite, Zūm Rails, VoPay** | Canadian, built for lenders and payments | Sales | One-off pulls | Rule out |

**Plaid wins on access, and access is the constraint that binds.** New US and Canadian teams created on or after 15 April 2026 get a free Trial plan with **10 Production Items** covering Transactions (including on-demand refresh), Balance, Liabilities and Investments. Plaid itself labels Trial and Pay-as-you-go "most appropriate for hobbyist use" ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/account/billing/)). Plaid's support pages say Trial needs no business registration, security questionnaire or sales process. That comes from a search snippet, because the page returned 403 ([Plaid support](https://support.plaid.com/hc/en-us/articles/39994173227159-What-is-the-Plaid-Trial-plan)). An Item is one institution login and can hold several accounts, so 10 covers a household. Each self-hoster would open their own team, which is the bring-your-own-keys pattern Sure already uses ([Sure docs](https://docs.sure.am/providers/plaid)). Plaid's data model maps almost one to one onto Manilla's requirements. `/transactions/sync` returns cursor-paged added, modified and removed lists. A posted transaction carries `pending_transaction_id`, and the pending one appears in `removed` within the same update ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data/)). `date` is already a `YYYY-MM-DD` string ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/)). Plaid is on the Bank of Canada's CDB advisory committee ([Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/regulatory-oversight/consumer-driven-banking/consumer-driven-banking-advisory-committee/)), and it argued publicly for a **tiered accreditation model** that does not create "significant barriers for smaller fintechs" ([Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/features/canada-prepares-for-open-banking/)). That is the outcome Manilla needs on the fourth-party question. Plaid's weaknesses are real. It has made no explicit commitment to seek accreditation, its Canadian reliability is currently the worst documented, and the Trial terms on personal or self-hosted use were not read.

**Flinks is the strongest Canadian player and the wrong commercial fit.** It is majority-owned by National Bank, says it will pursue accreditation as a third-party service provider, and runs bank-side FDX endpoints through its "Outbound" product ([Flinks](https://www.flinks.com/blog/open-banking-canada-2026-launch-fintech-institutions); [Flinks docs](https://docs.flinks.com/llms.txt)). FirstOntario Credit Union went live on Flinks' open banking in June 2026 ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/110098/firstontario-credit-union-activates-open-banking-with-fis-everlink-payment-services-and-flinks)). But Flinks charges **C$500 a month minimum on a one-year term**, with no self-serve trial and no pay-as-you-go ([Flinks pricing](https://www.flinks.com/pricing)). Its API is a poor fit as well. It returns **posted transactions only**, as a 90- or 365-day snapshot with no cursor ([Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/guides/getting-started/key-concepts)), and it does not document whether a transaction's `Id` stays the same across pulls. Its free Toolbox sandbox, with shared public credentials against a dummy "Flinks Capital" bank ([Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/guides/getting-started/testing-environments)), is still worth using as a second test target.

**MX is the sleeper.** It has the CIBC agreement and claims the broadest US and Canadian deposit coverage ([MX](https://www.mx.com/news/cibc-to-enhance-secure-and-seamless-access-to-financial-tools-and-apps-for-clients-through-agreement-with-mx/)). It sits underneath SimpleFIN, YNAB and Monarch, so it has the most consumer-app traffic in Canada after Plaid. Production access goes through sales. SimpleFIN is the hobby-priced way in, but its protocol is the thinnest of the candidates: 90-day windows, at most 24 requests a day, no delta or removal signal, and `posted` sent as a Unix epoch ([SimpleFIN](https://www.simplefin.org/protocol.html); [SimpleFIN](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/info/developers)). One 2026 blog post reports an MX bug in May 2026 that "caused account data to mix between users" ([DueZen](https://getduezen.com/blog/is-simplefin-safe/)). That claim is unconfirmed by a primary source, but it is a reminder that a consumer reseller adds a party to the chain.

The rule-outs are decisive. Every region-tagged endpoint in Mastercard's published open-finance spec is US-only ([Budgie research](https://github.com/RobertG-H/budgie-src/pull/45)). Yodlee's free sandbox "no longer supports linking to live sites", and its FAQ lists the US, UK and Australia ([Yodlee](https://developer.yodlee.com/resources/yodlee/faqs/docs/building_testing)). Akoya is a network owned by US banks. Inverite, Zūm Rails and VoPay sell underwriting-style pulls to lenders ([Inverite Insights](https://inveriteinsights.com/)).

For investments, which CDB phases in last, **Wealthica** covers 150 or more Canadian institutions and brokerages, but its keys and pricing are sales-only and most of its connections use credentials ([Newswire](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/unlocking-financial-connectivity-wealthica-launches-canada-s-most-comprehensive-financial-api-837576334.html); [Wealthica](https://wealthica.com/developers/)). Questrade's personal API ([Questrade](https://www.questrade.com/api)) and SnapTrade's free five-account tier ([SnapTrade](https://snaptrade.com/pricing)) are cheaper, non-scraping routes for the brokerages they cover. Whether a broker's own API breaks Manilla's rule is a separate decision, because no third party holds a password.

## What to build now: one sync core, two sandboxes, zero real connections

Everything below runs against synthetic data and touches no bank, so none of it breaks the decision. Following AGENTS.md, it belongs in `src/`, where the tests can reach it.

**An adapter boundary with a normalised line.** Each provider adapter emits the same record: provider, provider account id, provider transaction id, date as `YYYY-MM-DD`, amount as integer cents with inflow positive, a pending flag, the pending id a posted line replaces (if any), and description. Sign conventions differ in every direction. **Plaid's positive amount is money out** ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/)). MX and Yodlee send an unsigned amount plus a CREDIT/DEBIT type, SimpleFIN is signed with deposits positive, and Flinks splits each transaction into `Debit` and `Credit` ([Budgie research](https://github.com/RobertG-H/budgie-src/pull/45); [Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/api/connect/endpoints/account-linking/get-accounts-detail)). Every one of them sends amounts as JSON numbers, so a plain `JSON.parse` hands Manilla a float. The adapter should read each amount's source text, for example with a reviver that gets the raw number text or a number-preserving parser, and pass that text through `parseAmount`. Tests should pin the awkward values: `1234.56`, `0.1`, negative zero and amounts with no decimal places.

**Dates from the right field, with the timezone hazard isolated.** For Plaid, use `date`, which is the posting date once a transaction posts, and never derive a date from `datetime`. A transaction's date can change when it moves from pending to posted, and that can move it into a different budget month. SimpleFIN's epoch `posted` has no stated timezone ([SimpleFIN](https://www.simplefin.org/protocol.html)), which is the `20250903000000[-6:MDT]` hazard AGENTS.md describes. If a SimpleFIN adapter is ever written, convert in one function and check it against the OFX `DTPOSTED` of the same entries. FR-18 matching provides that cross-check for free.

**FR-18 merge as a proposal.** An aggregator id and an OFX FITID for the same bank entry will never be equal, so merging a synced line with a file-imported one needs a match on account, exact cents and a date window. Actual uses exact id first, then same amount within ±7 days, preferring the same payee ([Actual](https://actualbudget.org/docs/transactions/importing/)). Actual's bug history shows that provider ids are sometimes re-sent under a new id, which creates duplicates ([fork PR](https://github.com/azimul-kabir/actua/pull/799)), so the fuzzy fallback is needed, not optional. A unique exact match can attach the aggregator id as another `transaction_external_ids` row, which that table was designed for. An ambiguous match should be put to a person, consistent with "automation proposes; a person confirms".

**FR-19 pending as provisional, never confirmed.** Providers disagree here. Plaid links posted to pending. MX "most commonly" deletes the pending transaction and creates a new posted one with a new guid, and drops any pending transaction after 14 days. Yodlee mints a new id on posting by default. Flinks never shows pending at all ([Budgie research](https://github.com/RobertG-H/budgie-src/pull/45); [Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/guides/getting-started/key-concepts)). The safe design keeps pending lines out of the ledger, alongside suggestions. When a posted line arrives, it replaces its pending predecessor through the explicit link where one exists, or through the same fuzzy match where none does, accepting a changed amount such as a tip. Only posted lines become confirmed transactions, so a confirmed line never loses its source. Plaid's removed/added pair can span pages ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data/)), so apply a whole sync run in one database transaction, after `has_more` is false, and restart from the saved cursor on `TRANSACTIONS_SYNC_MUTATION_DURING_PAGINATION`. That keeps a half-applied sync from ever breaking the envelope-equals-account invariant.

**FR-20 connection storage and one-click revoke.** The schema has the `bank_sync` source and the `aggregator` id kind, but nothing yet stores a connection. `accounts.external_account_id` holds the OFX `ACCTID` and is globally unique, so it cannot also hold Plaid's account id. A connection table should hold the provider, the encrypted token (Plaid `access_token` or Flinks `loginId`), status and last successful sync. A per-account link should map each Manilla account to a provider account id. Prefix aggregator id values with the provider (for example `plaid:…`). The primary key on `transaction_external_ids` is (account, kind, value), and FR-15 lets an account change provider. Revoke has to call the provider, not just delete the row. Plaid's `/item/remove` is also what stops subscription billing ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/api/items/)). Flinks' `/DeleteCard` purges the credentials Flinks keeps indefinitely, and fails with `CARD_IN_USE` while a session is open ([Flinks](https://docs.flinks.com/api/connect/endpoints/account-linking/delete-card)).

**Polling, refresh and health on screen.** A home server should not depend on a public webhook endpoint. Sure moved away from a Plaid-only design partly because of that ([Sure discussion](https://github.com/we-promise/sure/discussions/93)). A daily scheduled `/transactions/sync` plus a user-triggered `/transactions/refresh` satisfies FR-16 ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/webhooks/)). Every account should show its data age and a "needs re-authentication" state, driven by Plaid's `ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED` and `PENDING_DISCONNECT` or SimpleFIN's `con.auth` error. Re-authentication must use Plaid Link **update mode**, which keeps the existing Item ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/link/update-mode/)). On Trial, deleting an Item and adding it again **burns one of the ten lifetime Items** ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/account/billing/)).

**Where to test.** Build the Plaid adapter against the Plaid Sandbox, which has test data and `/sandbox/item/fire_webhook`. Build a Flinks adapter against the Toolbox. Its posted-only snapshot model is the opposite of Plaid's deltas, and an abstraction that handles both will handle MX or a future FDX feed. Keep the fixtures synthetic, in `data/samples/`. Plaid warns that the sandbox "does not reflect institution-specific quirks" ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/sandbox/)). The Canadian MFA behaviour therefore cannot be exercised before going live, which is one more reason to make connection health a first-class screen. Opening a Plaid team now is harmless, and it secures Trial eligibility under today's rules. **Do not link a real institution on it**: that would be the scraping the decision forbids, and it would spend an Item.

## The signals that decide when to switch on

The gate for turning sync on should be **per account, not per app**. Manilla should refuse to link an account unless the provider says that this connection runs over the bank's API. Today the only signals are institution-level flags: Plaid's `oauth` and Flinks' `IsOauth`. Plaid's flag is `true` "if OAuth is supported for any Items… even if the institution also supports non-OAuth connections" ([Plaid](https://plaid.com/docs/api/institutions/)), so it cannot prove that a given Item avoided credentials. Watch for a connection-level or CDB-specific flag from the chosen provider. Without one, Manilla cannot enforce its own rule.

| Signal | Where it will show | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Final regulations in Gazette Part II | [Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2026/2026-06-27/html/reg3-eng.html), law-firm briefings | Starts the clock of up to a year until the rules are fully in force |
| Fourth-party / onward-disclosure rule | Final regulatory text, Bank of Canada guidelines | Whether Manilla can receive CDB data through Plaid at all |
| Credit-card classification | Product-scope schedule | Whether the first wave is useful for an envelope budget |
| Accreditation registry listing Plaid (or MX, Flinks) | [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/regulatory-oversight/consumer-driven-banking/) public registry | Which aggregator is eligible, not merely willing |
| Mandated-bank list and threshold | Ministerial designation | Which of the household's institutions are covered at launch |
| Standards-body designation (FDX expected) | Minister of Finance | Whether provider data models converge on FDX fields |
| Plaid Trial terms or pricing change | [Plaid billing docs](https://plaid.com/docs/account/billing/) | Whether a household still gets in free |
| Scraping-ban commencement | Finance Canada | When credential connections disappear from the market |

Three operational points come with going live. Express consent under CDB lasts **at most 12 months** ([Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/proposed-consumer-driven-banking-regulations-released-for-comment/)), so annual re-consent is built in and the health screen must warn before it lapses. CDB requires that at least 24 months of history be available on request ([Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations)), which makes the FR-18 merge against existing file imports the first and largest job any new connection does. And credit unions that opt in late, the investment accounts that come last, and any card that lands in the lending phase will all stay on file import well after chequing goes automatic. OFX import is not a stopgap that sync will retire; it stays a permanent path.

## Conclusion

The aggregator choice is less important than it looked. Every candidate reaches Canadian banks the same way today, and when CDB opens, the deciding questions will be regulatory ones that no vendor controls: whether an unaccredited app can receive framework data, and which phase cards fall in. Plaid is the right first target because it is the only one a household can use without a contract, and that only became true with its April 2026 Trial plan. The commitment should still be to the adapter boundary, not to Plaid.

Waiting therefore costs Manilla little in engineering terms. The hard parts of FR-17 to FR-20 are cents-exact parsing, merging with file imports, pending lines that disappear, and revocation. They can all be built and tested now against sandboxes and synthetic fixtures. The one capability Manilla does not have is a reliable per-connection signal that a link runs through the bank's API rather than a stored password. Asking Plaid and Flinks for that signal, and refusing to link without it, is how the "no scraping" decision should be enforced once sync exists.
